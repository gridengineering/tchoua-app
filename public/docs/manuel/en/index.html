Chapter 1

Vision, positioning and promise

Tchoua is not just a tontine calculator. It is a trust infrastructure for every form of tontine-type association: friendly societies, sections, federations, cooperatives, mutuals, women's groups, hometown associations.

1.1 The problem

In Cameroon and across much of Africa, the tontine is a savings and credit mechanism built on a spoken word, a notebook, a treasurer and collective memory. This model works as long as the group is small and members know one another. It weakens as soon as the association grows: records get lost, contributions are not reconciled, loans are not tracked, aid is granted without an objective basis, and distant sections do not know what is being decided at national level.

Existing tools are either spreadsheets or tontine apps limited to the rotating turn. None covers the reality of a friendly society such as the FEV: bylaws, a steering committee, sections, a national fund, illness and bereavement aid, distinctions, sanctions, quorums, terms of office and documents to be preserved.

1.2 The promise

Tchoua brings three inseparable things:

  • Transparency — every member sees their contributions, loans, aid, interest shares and balance; the board sees the whole picture; every movement is time-stamped and attributed.
  • Configurability — no rule is hard-coded: amounts, frequencies, quorums, caps, sanctions, cycles and allocation methods are configured per association and per activity.
  • Complete coverage — from creating the association to closing the cycle, including membership, meetings, tontines, loans, solidarity, reports and administration.

1.5 What sets Tchoua apart

Business depth. A tontine is not just a rotating turn: it is a contract with shares, fractions, sessions, a pot, auctions, beneficiaries, a balance and a closing. Tchoua implements these objects and their rules, including edge cases (insufficient pot, exhausted shares, defaulting member, multiple beneficiaries).

Governance. Bylaws and internal rules are not decorative: they become parameters (quorums, majorities, terms of office, sanctions) and enforceable documents, with version history and approvals.

Federation. A parent association can group sections together; each section keeps its own funds and local rules. This is the FEV / SLD model, transferable to all hometown associations.

Positioning

The reference platform for managing every form of tontine-type association in Cameroon and Africa: web, mobile, multilingual, multi-currency, configurable without code.

Chapter 2

Architecture, data and security

Tchoua is a modern web application, deployed in production and available at tchoua.com and www.tchoua.com. It is designed to serve several associations, several hierarchical levels and several currencies without duplicating code.

2.1 Overview

The application is built on Next.js (App Router), React, TypeScript, Prisma and PostgreSQL. Public pages and the member area share the same foundation; the APIs systematically verify association membership and role before any read or write. Files (bylaws, minutes, supporting documents) are stored on the server and referenced in the database.

2.3 Data model

The model is deliberately centred on the association. A user can belong to several associations with different roles. An association owns activities; each activity owns sessions, contributions, beneficiaries, loans and repayments. Documents and rule articles are attached to the association.

Figure 2.2 — Core entities and relationships. Full cardinalities are described in the repository's Prisma schema.

2.4 Security

  • Authentication — hashed passwords (bcrypt), signed JWT sessions, __Secure-next-auth.session-token cookie, expiry and renewal.
  • Authorization — two-level control: association membership and role (member, board, system administrator). Sensitive routes use a shared guard.
  • Least privilege — a regular member sees only their own operations; the board sees the whole picture; the system administrator does not stand in for the board on business decisions.
  • Audit — administrative actions and status changes are logged with author, date and reason. Financial movements are never deleted: corrections create reversing entries.
  • Transport — HTTPS mandatory, HTTP → HTTPS redirect, Let's Encrypt certificates renewed automatically.
  • Files — controlled types and sizes (PDF, JPEG, PNG, WebP; 10 MB), access by role.
Warning

Credentials, session secrets, API keys and passwords must never appear in the interface, screenshots or documents. Demo accounts are disabled in production.

2.5 Multi-association and hierarchy

Each association is a sealed space: its members, activities, cash boxes, documents and reports. An association can have a parent (federation, national friendly society) and children (sections). Roles are evaluated at the level of the association concerned; a member can be a regular member in one section and a national board member.

This architecture can serve both a neighbourhood tontine of ten members and a national federation of several thousand members, without changing code or database.

2.6 Operations and continuity

Production is supervised: PM2 processes, HAProxy reverse proxy, TLS certificates, database backups and logging. Deployments are automated by a script that aligns the repository, regenerates the Prisma client, builds the application and restarts the process. SQL migrations are idempotent and applied before the restart.

3.4 Account lifecycle

  1. Registration — name, e-mail, password, acceptance of terms; or invitation by the board.
  2. Verification — the account is active after e-mail validation or activation by an administrator.
  3. Membership — the member joins an association via a code, an invitation or a request approved by the board.
  4. Activation — the board assigns the role and member number; the join date is recorded.
  5. Account life — contributions, loans, aid, votes, documents, notifications.
  6. Exit — resignation, removal or death; the status changes to LEFT, entitlements are calculated and the history is retained.
Information

A member can belong to several associations with different roles. Their dashboard aggregates their commitments, but each association keeps its own accounting and rules.

3.5 Profile settings

Each user manages their profile, password, language, member-area theme and notifications. Appearance preferences are stored server-side and applied as soon as the page loads, without flicker.

3.6 Good practice

  • One account per person, never a shared account.
  • The board never shares its password; rights are granted by role.
  • Sensitive functions (membership approval, disbursement, closing) are traced with the author.
  • Inactive or departing accounts are disabled, not deleted, to preserve the history.
  • System administrators are few, and their actions are audited.
Chapter 4

Association lifecycle

Creating an association is not just entering a name. You have to define its identity, its affiliation, its board, its membership conditions, its meetings, its aid, its sanctions and its internal rules — then bring it to life.

4.1 The six steps

1. Creation

The creator enters the name, the type (friendly society, section, federation, tontine, cooperative, mutual…), the region, the colour, the logo, the description, the possible parent association and the starting template. A unique invitation code is generated.

2. Configuration

The board configures how the association operates: bodies, quorums, majorities, terms of office, membership conditions, joining fee, annual contribution, meeting frequency, aid catalogue, sanctions, bank accounts. These settings are stored as JSON and remain editable.

3. Membership

Candidates complete an application, provide their information and are sponsored where the rule requires it. The board approves or rejects; activation records the join date, the member number and the role.

4. Activities

The association creates its activities: rotating tontine, ASCA tontine, auction tontine, savings, investment, loan fund, solidarity fund, catering, group purchases, in-kind contributions.

5. Sessions

Each activity schedules its sessions according to its frequency. Members contribute, attendance is recorded, fines are applied, beneficiaries are designated, loans are disbursed and repaid.

6. Closing

At the end of the cycle, the association produces its statement, calculates dividends, archives the documents and switches to read-only. A new cycle can be created without erasing the history.

4.3 Two-level governance

The FEV / SLD model shows the power of hierarchy: the FEV is a national friendly society with a general assembly, a steering committee, an executive board, committees and sections. The SLD is the Douala section, with its own committee, its own rules and its own funds.

Figure 4.2 — Actual FEV / SLD organisation: national GA, steering committee, board, committees, Douala section.

Each level has its own rules: the national GA meets once a year with a quorum of half the members; the steering committee decides by a 2/3 majority; the Douala section meets on the first Saturday after the 5th, with a quorum of 3/5. Tchoua stores these parameters and surfaces them in the governance screens.

4.4 Bylaws, internal rules and documents

The bylaws and the internal rules are structured into numbered articles that members can consult and approve. The original documents (PDF, DOCX) can be downloaded from the association record. Versions are retained and changes are traced.

4.5 Special cases

  • Section without legal personality — it inherits the parent's bylaws and manages its own funds. Tchoua models it as a child association.
  • Federation — it groups autonomous associations; consolidation of headcounts and funds is described as a future development.
  • Dormant association — SUSPENDED status, consultation possible, entries frozen.
  • Dissolution — remaining assets are allocated according to the bylaws; the history remains consultable.
Golden rule

An association with movements is never deleted: it is closed. The history is the association's memory and the proof of its transparency.

6.3 Subscription and shares

An active member subscribes between the minimum fraction and any applicable cap. Their expected contribution at each session is their shares multiplied by the share amount. If they subscribe several shares, their contribution is multiplied accordingly; their beneficiary entitlement follows the same proportion.

expected contribution = subscribed shares × share amount
beneficiary entitlement = beneficiary shares × amount per beneficiary share

6.4 The pot and distributable shares

A session's pot is the sum of the contributions collected, any carry-over and approved adjustments. The number of distributable shares is the smaller of two numbers: the cap on shares per session, and the number of shares the pot can finance.

pot = Σ contributions collected + carry-over + adjustments
distributable shares = min(session cap, floor(pot ÷ amount per share))
cycle balance = Σ contributions − Σ distributions
Warning

If the pot is insufficient, the distribution may be blocked, partial or deferred according to the activity's rule. No distribution may make the balance negative without explicit authorisation of a cash advance.

A beneficiary's eligibility takes into account their active status, contributions up to date, remaining shares and any previous receipts. A member can receive several shares in the same session if the activity allows it and within the cap.

6.7 Auctions at the SLD

The SLD runs an annual tontine of 25 000 FCFA and its multiples, allocated by public auction with a starting bid of 5% of the contribution rate. The member who wins the auction receives the pot minus the discount; the interest generated is distributed in lots or carried over. Defaulting members incur a 15% surcharge if they have not yet benefited and 20% if they have already benefited.

6.8 Worked examples

Example A — 12 sessions, 1 share, 12 members

Share at 50 000 FCFA, amount per beneficiary share 550 000 FCFA, one beneficiary per session. Each session collects 12 × 50 000 = 600 000 FCFA, distributes 550 000 FCFA and leaves a balance of 50 000 FCFA. After 12 sessions, all 12 members have benefited and the cumulative balance is 600 000 FCFA.

Example B — multiple shares

Four members with 2 shares and four members with 1 share represent 12 shares. With two beneficiary shares per session, the cycle ends in six sessions.

Example C — share exhaustion

20 shares, one distributable share per session, variable pot. Session 1 distributes one share, session 2 may distribute two if the pot allows; the cycle stops when all 20 shares have a beneficiary.

Example D — half share

Share at 100 000 FCFA, member with 0.5 share: contribution of 50 000 FCFA and beneficiary entitlement of 0.5 × the amount per share.

7.2 Lifecycle of a contribution

  1. Call — the open session generates the expected contributions from the subscribed shares.
  2. Collection — the treasurer enters the amount, the method (cash, Mobile Money, transfer, in-kind) and the reference.
  3. Status — PAID, PARTIAL, LATE, EXEMPT. The status determines eligibility and reminders.
  4. Reconciliation — the amount is assigned to the activity's cash box and to the session pot.
  5. Reminder — unpaid amounts trigger a notification and, if configured, a fine.
  6. Closing — the session is frozen; later corrections create an adjustment.
expected = shares × share amount
collected = Σ validated payments
outstanding = expected − collected

7.5 Catering

Catering is a reception fund: each member contributes a fixed amount per session, and the member hosting the meeting receives a flat fee, after deduction of their arrears. At the SLD, the contribution is 3 000 FCFA and the flat fee 150 000 FCFA.

7.6 Activities and contribution calls

Each activity card shows the contribution amount, the frequency, the available cash box, the number of subscribers and the actions (view, contribute, manage). Mandatory activities appear in the member area with the expected amount and the due date.

7.7 Reminders and automation

A scheduled job identifies late contributions, loan deadlines and upcoming sessions, then sends notifications. The job can be run in simulation mode to check the recipients before the actual send. Reminders are logged to avoid duplicates.

Information

Notification channels (e-mail, WhatsApp, SMS) are configured at platform level. The content and language follow the member's preferences.

7.8 Treasury good practice

  • Enter contributions the same day, never at the end of the month.
  • Attach the Mobile Money reference or the receipt to each payment.
  • Have corrections approved by a second person.
  • Close the session before opening the next one.
  • Export the cash journal at the end of every month.
  • Reconcile the Tchoua balance and the bank balance before the GA.

7.9 Session closing checks

  • Cap — fixed amount, percentage of the cash box or multiple of the member's stake.
  • Rate — monthly rate, possibly decreasing after a few months.
  • Term — number of months and repayment schedule.
  • Guarantees — savings, surety, guarantor, cheque, acknowledgement of debt.
  • Approval — board, secretariat or automatic depending on the amount and the configuration.
  • Simultaneous active loans — maximum number per member.
  • Arrears — penalty, surcharge, suspension, recovery.

instalment = principal ÷ term + interest for the period
cap = min(available cash box, member cap, multiple of the stake)

8.3 Loan cycle

  1. The member creates an application: amount, purpose, term, proposed guarantees.
  2. The board reviews it: check of the cash box, the cap, active loans and repayment capacity.
  3. The application is approved or rejected with reasons; the decision is notified.
  4. The disbursement is recorded (cash, Mobile Money, transfer) and the cash box is debited.
  5. Repayments are entered with principal and interest separated.
  6. The loan is settled; the interest feeds the allocation and the dividends.

8.4 Interest allocation

Each repayment includes a principal portion and an interest portion. Interest is allocated among members in proportion to the capital they held on the loan's disbursement date. This key prevents a member who joined after the loan from receiving interest they did not help to generate.

share = member's capital on the loan date ÷ total capital on the loan date
interest share = interest repaid × share

The "Interest" screen details, for each repayment: the date, the borrower, the activity, the total interest, the member's capital, the overall capital, the share and the amount due to them. The board can expand the allocation across all members.

Chapter 19

ARP framework: rules, combinations and versions

Since November 2026, Tchoua no longer merely records activities: each association has an ARP framework (Association Resource Planning) that describes its form, its rules, its authorised modules and its compatibility conditions. This framework is versioned, checked and enforceable against new operations.

19.1 Why an ARP framework

A neighbourhood tontine, a family friendly society, a local branch and a national federation do not have the same bodies, the same funds or the same obligations. The ARP framework separates what must be separated:

  • the user account and the membership;
  • the expected contribution (receivable), the payment (treasury) and the allocation (imputation);
  • the fund (authorised use) and the account (place of safekeeping);
  • the decision to allocate and the disbursement;
  • the published rule and the historical operations, which keep their version.
Principle

The ARP framework does not replace the articles or the internal rules. It makes them executable: a declared rule becomes a control, a mandatory field or an explicit refusal. The software model is worth neither filed articles nor authorisation to carry on a regulated activity.

19.2 Thirty-six domains, one hundred and forty-four invariants

The ARP reference covers 36 domains: identity, federations, memberships, families, governance, meetings, discipline, documents, rules engine, contributions, payments, cash boxes, tontines, allocations, auctions, cycles, savings, loans, schedules, repayments, guarantees, solidarity, aid, reserves, investments, projects, purchases, assets, events, community, reports, security, architecture, mobile, operations and internationalisation.

19.5 Twenty-three thousand combinations, a single logic

The ten dimensions produce 23 328 combinations. The engine does not store these rows: it computes the conditions from the values and checks each configuration with a test recipe. This approach avoids frozen catalogues and guarantees that a new value remains compatible with the existing controls.

conditions = C01(dimension nature + tontine) ∪ C02(auction + offline) ∪ C03(mobile money + offline) ∪ C04(conversion) ∪ C05(family + aid) ∪ C06(credit + single validation) qualification = "conditional" if conditions ≠ ∅ "no additional condition" otherwise

19.6 Profile, draft, publication and versions

Each association has a single ARP profile but several versions. The active profile carries the configuration applied to new operations; the draft allows a change to be prepared without interrupting the existing one.

StateMeaningEffect
DRAFTUnpublished profileNew financial subscriptions are blocked until publication
ACTIVEPublished versionModules and conditions are applied
BACKFILLProfile imported for an existing associationContinues to operate; to be reviewed and published by the board
SUSPENDED / ARCHIVEDFramework suspended or archivedViewing and liquidation possible, new subscriptions closed

A publication creates an immutable version with a SHA-256 fingerprint. Old operations remain attached to their rule version; a retroactive correction never rewrites the history, it creates a linked operation in an open period.

19.7 Funds, movements and reservations

The financial core distinguishes the fund (economic allocation), the movement (confirmed inflow or outflow) and the reservation (a commitment that protects the available balance without changing the balance). The available balance is computed as follows:

available = balance (confirmed inflows − confirmed outflows) − active reservations
  • An outflow can never exceed the available balance; the check is performed under a concurrent lock.
  • A reservation can be released (returned to the available balance) or consumed (creating a linked disbursement).
  • Amounts are kept in whole minor units: XAF without decimals, EUR in cents. No financial calculation uses binary floating point.
  • Disabling a module hides new subscriptions but preserves the viewing, corrections and liquidations required by existing obligations.

19.8 Simulators and proof of calculations

Three simulators make the rules verifiable before any entry is posted: loan schedule (constant principal, constant annuity, bullet), tontine allocation (pot, session cap, remaining entitlements, minimum fraction) and social fund projection (headcount, contribution, collection rate).

19.9 Administration and coverage

The administration console shows the ARP coverage of all associations: status, origin, version, completeness, errors, alerts, active modules and number of funds. It can initialise missing profiles from existing activities, without interrupting production.

Accepted limitations

The ARP framework is the anchor point for future developments. The modules not yet built — double-entry accounting journal, guarantees register, payment connectors, projects and inventory, real-time auctions, federation consolidation — remain identified workstreams and are not presented as finished.

The Contributions tab does not create an activity: it sets the cross-cutting parameters that feed the calls. It holds the basis (fixed, shares, in-kind), the entry fee, the minimum age, the admission mode, the sponsors, the waiting period, the family cap unit and the household definition.

When the basis is "In-kind", the C01 fields appear: valuation method and liquidity rule. An in-kind contribution does not automatically become cash; it must be sold or funded before distribution.

21.5 Meetings and catering

The Meetings & catering tab sets the frequency, day, time, quorum, contribution per member and the flat rate paid back to the host. These parameters are distinct from financial sessions: a meeting can cover several activities, and an activity can run without a physical meeting.

21.6 Funds and reservations

The ARP page contains the Funds & reservations tab. It allows several funds to be created (unrestricted, restricted, reserve, refundable deposit), inflows and outflows to be recorded, and an amount to be reserved for an aid, an allocation or a guarantee.

21.7 Versions, audit and control

Each save of the ARP framework creates a draft. Publication turns that draft into an active, immutable version, with a SHA-256 fingerprint. Versions can be viewed in the Versions tab; old operations keep the rule that applied on their date.

  • Activity changes are logged with the author and the date.
  • Financial parameters are never corrected by deletion: a linked operation keeps the trace.
  • Disabling a module closes new subscriptions but lets repayments and liquidations continue.
  • The board can return to an earlier version by publishing a new version, never by overwriting the history.

21.8 Configuration checklist

  • The name, type and country are filled in.
  • The functional currency is correct and the time zone matches the head office.
  • The ARP dimensions match the activities actually open.
  • Conditions C01–C06 are complete or explicitly accepted as still to be completed.
  • Each activity has a consistent status, participation mode and frequency.
  • Caps, rates and penalties have been reviewed by the treasurer.
  • The internal rules are up to date and members can approve them.
  • Funds and reservations are created before the first disbursements.
